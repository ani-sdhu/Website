Description

I study natural gas price volatility and the decisions of state public service commissions. I use time-series volatility models, vote-level regression, and text analysis.

I am a first-year Ph.D. student in Political Science and International Affairs at the University of Georgia, on the methodology track with an energy emphasis. I work as an Energy Analyst at UGA's Benson-Bertsch Center for International Trade and Security. I have also worked in the energy industry, on LNG and oil market risk and on a land drilling rig refurbishment project.

Research

In progress

Partisanship and decision-making on state public service commissions

Vote-level regression with vote fixed effects

I compare votes on elected partisan commissions, appointed commissions with party-balance rules, and appointed commissions without such rules. Vote-level regressions with vote fixed effects estimate the partisan gap.

Weather shocks and natural gas price volatility: a five-state panel

EGARCH, EGARCH-X, HAR-RV

EGARCH and EGARCH-X models for Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Maryland, and Ohio, with heating and cooling degree day surprises as exogenous regressors. A companion HAR-RV adaptation uses daily Henry Hub spot prices.

The strategic deployment of rhetoric by states

Natural language processing

I use natural language processing to measure how states use rhetoric strategically, and I test whether that rhetoric helps predict political violence. Presented at the Southeast Undergraduate Research Conference, Athens, GA, 2023.

Master's capstone

The Impact of Communication on U.S.-Saudi Gas Market Shock and Interaction
